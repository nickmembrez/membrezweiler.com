Run the script

The script uses the General Social Survey (GSS), which has interviewed national samples of American adults since 1972. This version covers 2000 to 2014 and includes 21,483 people. The script asks one question: what share of American adults report no religion, and how did that share change?

Before you run it: in 2014, what percent of American adults in the GSS reported no religion?
10%

Now run it

  1. Click anywhere inside the script.
  2. Click the Source button at the top right of the script pane.
  3. Wait a few seconds. The chart appears in the Plots tab, lower right.
Checkpoint. Your chart should look like the one below, with the title "Religious non-affiliation rose in the United States." If it does not, look at the table of common problems further down, or ask the person next to you.

What the script just did

  1. Step 1 loaded three packages. A package is a set of tools that other people wrote. One of them contains the GSS data.
  2. Step 2 showed you the data in the Console. Each row is one person, and each column is one survey question, called a variable.
  3. Step 3 calculated, for each survey year, the share of people whose answer to the religion question was "None," and drew those shares as a line.

If something goes wrong

What you seeWhat to do
"there is no package called"The computer is missing a package. Step 0 of the script installs it; wait for it to finish, then click Source again. If it still fails, type install.packages("ggplot2") in the Console, using the package name from the message, and press Enter.
"could not find function"Click Source again so the script runs from the top. The library() lines have to run first.
"object not found"Check spelling and capital letters. R treats Gss and gss as different names.
A + sign in the Console and nothing happensA quotation mark or parenthesis is missing. Click in the Console, press Escape, fix the line, and click Source again.
No chart appearsMake sure the line print(my_chart) is still in the script.

Change it: a guided step

Code is something you can change. In this step you change one word and the labels, and the script makes a different chart.

  1. In Step 3 of the script, find this line:
    summarize(share = mean(relig == "None"))
  2. Change "None" to "Protestant". Keep the quotation marks. The line should now read:
    summarize(share = mean(relig == "Protestant"))
  3. Change the title, the subtitle, and the y-axis label (y = ) so that they describe the new chart. Keep the quotation marks around each label.
  4. Click Source again.
Checkpoint. Your new line should fall, as in the chart below. Your title will use your own words. Check that your title and labels say Protestant, not "no religion."
One group's share rose and another's fell. Keep this in mind for question 3 on the response sheet: does this chart show you that individual people left one religion for another?

On your own

This step has no example to copy. You decide what to chart, write your own labels, and write one line of code yourself.

  1. Pick a group. When you clicked Source, the Console printed a list of every religious group in the data and the number of people in each. Pick any group other than None or Protestant. Change the group name in Step 3 to your group. Type it exactly as it appears in the list, with the same capital letters, inside quotation marks.
  2. Write your own labels. Change the title, subtitle, and y-axis label to describe your chart.
  3. Save your chart as a picture. At the bottom of the script, under the comment "Write your ggsave() line here," write one line that saves your chart. Use the ggsave() example on your R reference sheet. Name the file with your last name, for example smith_chart.png.
  4. Click Source. Open the Files tab in the lower right. Your picture file should be there.
Two notes. A group with few people in the survey gives a jagged line that jumps up and down from year to year. That comes from small numbers, not necessarily from real change. Also, some group names in this 2000 to 2014 data use older spellings. Type each name exactly as it appears in the list, because R matches the text letter for letter.
Before you leave: save the script in your SOCL3633 folder (File, then Save). You will submit the picture file with your response sheet.
