The conventions

Use exactly these conventions, so that everyone's transcripts can be read and coded the same way in Lab 11.

What happensWhat you type
Each turn of talkI: for you (the interviewer) and R: for the respondent, at the start of each turn
A noticeable pause(.)
A long pauseThe length in seconds, such as (3s)
Sounds that are not wordsIn square brackets: [laughs], [sighs], [phone buzzes]
Words you cannot make out(inaudible). Do not guess silently.
"Um," "like," "you know," false starts, and self-correctionsType them all. Do not clean up the talk.
Any real name of a person, employer, or place that could identify someoneA label in square brackets: [name], [manager], [employer], [town]

An example

I: So what was your first week like? R: Um, it was, [laughs] it was a lot. Like, I didn't, I didn't know anybody, and my, uh, my manager, [name], she just kind of (.) threw me on register. I: Mm-hm. R: And I was like, okay. (3s) I mean, I figured it out.

Practice: what was lost?

Someone transcribed the same exchange but cleaned it up. Compare the two versions. For each item, decide whether the cleaned version made that change.

Word for word

I: So what was your first week like? R: Um, it was, [laughs] it was a lot. Like, I didn't, I didn't know anybody, and my, uh, my manager, Tasha, she just kind of (.) threw me on register. I: Mm-hm. R: And I was like, okay. (3s) I mean, I figured it out.

Cleaned up

R: My first week was a lot. I didn't know anybody, and my manager, Tasha, put me on the register right away. I figured it out.

Transcribe

  1. Choose the 10 minutes of your recording that tell you the most about the respondent's job. This is often not the first 10 minutes. Write down the start and end times.
  2. Start the timer below, then transcribe those 10 minutes word for word, with your own questions included. Play a few seconds, pause, type, and replay as often as you need.
  3. Stop the timer when you finish, or when class ends. If you finish at home, start it again there.

The time fills in question 5 of the response sheet.
Researchers often plan on 3 to 4 minutes of typing for every minute of recording. If you are slow at first, that is normal.

Mark two moments

As you transcribe, put an asterisk (*) at the start of two places in your transcript:

  1. Moment 1: a place where the meaning depends on tone, pace, or laughter, and the words alone do not carry it.
  2. Moment 2: a place where you wanted to correct what the respondent said, and did not.
Checkpoint. Before you go further, check that your first page uses I: and R:, and that every real name is replaced with a label in brackets.
