Your plan, built in class

Fill in the printed plan and tally sheet (SOCL3633_Lab12_PlanAndTallySheet on D2L) in class. Your instructor initials it before you leave. Do not observe without an initialed plan.

  1. Setting. A public place where people gather and no one expects privacy: a coffee shop, a library floor, a food court, a gym lobby, a park, a laundromat. Not a private home, not a classroom during class, and not a place where drinking alcohol is the main activity. You observe for 30 minutes from one spot.
  2. Concept. One concept from your proposal topic that can show up in public behavior.
  3. Indicators. Three or four specific behaviors that will count as your concept. Each one must pass the test from today's activity: a stranger with your tally sheet would get nearly the same count. "Being friendly" is not an indicator. "Starts a conversation with someone they did not arrive with" is.
  4. How your indicators could mislead. Write two things: a behavior that looks like your concept but is not, and a way your concept could be present but invisible.
Checkpoint. Before you ask for initials, read each indicator aloud to the person next to you. If they ask "what counts as that?", add a rule to the definition.

Rules for observing

These follow the class-project rules from week 3. They are not optional.

In the field: 30 minutes

  1. Choose one spot where you can see without being in the way. Write down the date, the start time, and where you are sitting.
  2. Tally each indicator in three 10-minute columns on your tally sheet.
  3. Keep open fieldnotes on a separate page: what the place is like, what surprised you, and anything your tally sheet had no row for.
  4. Write up your fieldnotes within 24 hours, while you still remember the details.
  5. Take clear photos of your plan and tally sheet. Type or photograph your fieldnotes.
Thirty minutes is short. You will miss things, and that is part of what the response sheet asks about.
