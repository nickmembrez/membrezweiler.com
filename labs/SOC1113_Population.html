The Three Drivers SOC 1113 · Trend Lab · Midwestern State University

Every population story on Earth reduces to three numbers: births, deaths, and moves. This page holds the published versions of all three, plus the two forecasts they add up to: the shape of the age pyramid and the size of the century. Demography is the rare corner of sociology that genuinely predicts, because the people who will be middle-aged in 2060 have already been born. Guess before each reveal. The gaps between your guesses and the projections are mostly the gap between the population panic you have absorbed and what demographers actually expect.

Births: the engine that is winding down

The mechanism, and it works everywhere: as child survival rises and women gain education and opportunity, families choose fewer children. No exceptions have appeared on any continent. Worldwide, women now have one child fewer on average than around 1990. That single fact is why the explosion levels off, and it is the answer to the opening poll.

Sources: United Nations, World Population Prospects 2024 (global fertility 2.25 in 2024, down from 3.31 in 1990; country counts as stated). CDC/NCHS, Births: Final Data for 2023 (U.S. total fertility rate about 1.62). Bars scaled to 7.

Stage two of the transition, explained. Medicine, food, and sanitation cut deaths decades before birth rates followed. The gap between fast-falling deaths and still-high births is the entire population boom: not the end state of humanity, just the middle of a transition that births are now closing from the other side.

Read the shapes. Nigeria is a wide-based triangle: enormous young cohorts, fast growth ahead. Japan is top-heavy: three in ten people are 65 or older and one in nine is a child, so shrinkage is already arithmetic. The United States is a triangle turning into a column, aging steadily with the gray share doubled since 1960. The shape is the forecast: schools, labor markets, housing, health care, and the Social Security ratio in the tile above all follow from it, decades in advance.

Moves: the fastest driver

Births and deaths change a population slowly. Migration can reshape its size and composition within a decade, which is why demographers watch it hardest.

Why this number matters demographically. With U.S. fertility at 1.62, well below replacement, immigration is what keeps the population and the workforce growing, and what helps support the aging structure on the previous tab. Whatever position anyone holds politically, the demographic accounting is the same arithmetic for everyone: a below-replacement country grows through migration or not at all. Note the longer story too: today's share is near the level of the 1890s. The country has been here before.

Sources: Census Bureau data as compiled in published series: foreign-born share 14.8% in 1890, 4.7% in 1970, and 15.6% (51.6 million people) as of March 2024. Bars scaled to 30%.
