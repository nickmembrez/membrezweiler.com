Build and Launch Your Survey SOCL 3633 Research Methods · Lab 8 · Midwestern State University

Today you build a 10-question survey in Google Forms and send it to your first respondents. The first three questions are the same for everyone in the class, so that one R script can read every student's data in Lab 9. Questions 4 to 10 are yours, on your proposal topic. Work through the tabs in order. Before you leave, your form is published and sent, and your response sheet is pasted into the Lab 8 submission on D2L.

Keep this page open until you submit. Your entries are not saved anywhere. If you close or reload the page, they are lost.

Get set up

The required structure

Questions 1 to 3
The same for everyone in the class, in this order, with the exact wording and answer choices on the next tab.
Questions 4 to 10
Seven questions of your own on your proposal topic. All multiple choice, one answer each.
Optional comment box
One optional open question at the very end, such as "Is there anything else you would like to add?" It is not analyzed.
If your CITI certificates are not on D2L, build the form today but do not send it. You will use the practice data file in Lab 9 instead of your own responses.

Questions 1 to 3

Build these three first, exactly as written. The Lab 9 script expects these questions in these positions, so a changed word or answer choice can break it.

  1. In your form, click the first question. Set the question type to Multiple choice.
  2. Use the copy button, then paste the question into the question box. Type each answer choice as its own option, in the order shown.
  3. Leave Required turned off. Your consent text tells people they may skip any question.
  4. Click the plus button to add the next question, and repeat.
Checkpoint. Ask the person next to you to compare your three questions with this tab, word for word and choice for choice.

Questions 4 to 10

Add seven questions on your proposal topic, starting with your repaired questions from Lab 7. At least two of them must measure core concepts in your research question. You describe each question in the operationalization table on the Response sheet tab.

Put easy, general questions before specific ones. An early question can shape how people answer a later one, as the week 7 split-ballot example showed for question wording.

Consent text

Your respondents read this before they answer. It covers what the MSU Texas guidelines for class projects require: who you are, that this is a class project, the age requirement, what people will do and how long it takes, what happens to their answers, and how to reach you and the instructor.
